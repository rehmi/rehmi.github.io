Wearable Computing

The Borgs

The MIT "Borgs" were the Media Lab's wearable-computing group in the mid-1990s — a handful of people living in always-on computers worn on the body, read through a heads-up display and typed on a one-handed Twiddler. The independently built machine that led to joining them grew into HackMan 0.4, whose page tells that arrival story.

What living in a computer teaches

Wearing a computer all day is different from carrying one, and the difference is where the research was. Daily use surfaced the problems that came to define the field: power and heat on a body, one-handed input, a display you look through rather than at, and the social fact of holding a conversation with someone who is also reading a screen.

One conclusion from those years runs through the rest of this portfolio: the machine should disappear into the clothing itself. That is the road that leads to e-broidery and the garments built with it.
